## 1. Who Jun is (0:00 to 0:08)

Six years of creating, 8M followers, 10M USD in the last 12 months, worked with Meta, ManyChat, Red Bull. Grew up poor, immigrant from Korea, biomedical engineering student. His point: his life was not more interesting than ours; he learned to tell it. He also posted daily for about 15 months in a 200 view jail and lost followers while copying trends and niche advice (0:10). Growth came only after he started telling his own story.

## 2. Definition and the three depths (0:17 to 0:23)

Storytelling: taking something you have already lived and telling it so people feel it and see themselves in you. Everything we remember (books, songs, brands, films) is remembered because it made us feel something; his examples are Disney films reminding him of childhood, Korean street food reminding him of his sister's cooking, Coldplay's Yellow because a school bus driver hummed it.

The worst creators are anti-human: they say the same thing an AI could say.

Three depths of telling the same moment: safe is generic and like AI, real is relatable (the emotion under it), raw is engaging (the part you do not say out loud). Safe will never win; the best creators live in raw.

## 3. The storytelling wheel, L.I.F.E. (0:21 to 0:24)

Four pillars where stories come from: Love (relationships, hobbies), Identity (origin, culture, sex, age, faith), Fitness (mind, body, spirit, routines), Earnings (career, clients, skills; unemployment counts too). The wheel does two things: helps you find stories, and helps you find meaning by moving from the outer ring (safe) through real to raw at the centre. You place the moment outside the wheel, even the smallest one, and dig inwards.

Slide text: You are not one topic. You are made of 4 pillars.

## 4. The three whys (0:24 to 0:27)

Take the moment and ask why three times: the obvious reason, the feeling underneath, the part you do not say out loud.

His example: I drove my kid to school. Obvious: I want them on time, education matters. Underneath: I love being present and hearing their life updates. Unsaid: I am finally choosing to be present with them over work. The last one is the post; lots of parents feel it and will recognise themselves.

Second example: I recorded a video today. Obvious: I want a following. Underneath: I am scared nobody will care. Unsaid: even a hundred views means I did something I was scared to do. That turns into a post about never underestimating the impact of a story on even one person.

## 5. Problem, pursuit, payoff (0:28 to 0:39)

Every story has a beginning, middle and end; Jun names them problem, pursuit, payoff. He runs The Lion King, Titanic and Toy Story through it: different themes, the same three acts.

- Problem: whatever you have endured. Lead with it because struggle is what all people share (insecurity, fear of being seen, impostor syndrome, guilt). If you make someone care right away, they stay.
- Pursuit: your unique way of solving it, your method. This is how you build intellectual property and what people later buy (his first product was the method from his student content).
- Payoff: the outcome, insight or lesson. It does not mean success. Not having resolved something is a payoff in itself: a new realisation, a new awareness, one step in the right direction. Zoom into the moment.

His student example (0:31): one of 300 biomedical students, half dropped out (problem); worked twice as hard and built a learning system, quitting was not an option (pursuit); graduated without sacrificing other interests (payoff).

The pursuit can live in the caption (Michelle's video, 0:33). In a talking video the pursuit can be a list of steps with the personal story embedded in each (basketball player, 0:34).

## 6. Retention (0:39 to 0:40)

Retention graphs: content that reads like a list of facts, not a journey, drops sharply because viewers do not know where it is heading. A strong problem hook and an arc give a slower decline that flattens later. Opening with I have the coolest watch, I am perfect gives nothing to resonate with.

## 7. No arc versus 3P arc (0:40 to 0:44)

- Kadim, back to school fits brand deal. No arc: him rapping in new clothes, flat, no meaning for the audience. With arc: you are spending 200 dollars on one back to school fit, I ran out of fits in the first week when I was in college, here are affordable places. Same brand, same outcome, now a story.
- Janae, slide No arc 18.5K views versus 3P arc 360K views. No arc: getting ready to put get a boyfriend on my vision board for the third year in a row. Clear relatable problem, no pursuit, no payoff, so the video is one note and has no value. 3P arc: I am 27 and never had a boyfriend, not even a situationship, I wondered if I am worth being loved (problem); my life was not empty, it was unassigned, I was waiting for love to give me purpose instead of building a life love could walk into (pursuit); I stopped asking why has he not come yet and started asking who am I becoming while I wait (payoff); follow me if you are done wasting your waiting season (CTA).

## 8. Common mistakes slide (0:43:50)

- Problem is too vague: just saying I struggled is generic, which gives less to connect to.
- Payoff with no specificity: everything worked out is forgettable; I stopped pretending I wasn't scared is not.
- Skipping the pursuit: jumping straight from problem to payoff can feel like a flex; people need to understand the journey you went on to get there.

## 9. Formats (0:45 to 1:02)

Slide: Once you master the 3 Ps, you can package them into a format. And once it's a format, it's repeatable. A format is the style you choose to deliver the message (talking head, B-roll plus text, carousel). The topic is what you are saying, the format is how you are saying it.

- Silent film: his most popular format, about 100M views in a year, copied by Alex Hormozi (pinned post, 4.8M followers) and Shelby Sapp. See section 10.
- Split-screen reel (@rebeccacherry): each frame has the problem phrase on top (being an entrepreneur is hard, being a mom is hard) and the payoff phrase on the bottom (being an entrepreneur is empowering, being a mom is beautiful).
- Advice I needed at 22, not 30 (carousel): the problem is the first frame (she needed this advice earlier), the pursuit is the advice in between, the payoff is the last frame plus the CTA and caption.
- Old me vs new me: problem at the start, payoff at the end, pursuit in the caption.
- Problem statement: problem in the first line or first photo, pursuit in between, payoff at the end.
- Split-screen carousel (@itsaaroncampos): frames 1 to 5 have the problem on top and its result on the bottom (I started saying no and I lost friendships; I started respecting myself and I felt lonely), frame 6 is the overall payoff (putting yourself first isn't selfish, it's necessary), frame 7 the CTA, the pursuit is in the caption.

## 10. Silent film, shot by shot (0:47 to 0:53)

What the screen shows, from the frames:

- Problem lines, each over its own shot whose mood matches the line, not the literal event: 2013: Parents divorced abruptly... / and my mom left for Korea without a goodbye... / Struggled mentally and held a lot of resentment / Dad did his best but we had our worst years financially / I was embarrassed so I didn't tell anyone... / I struggled alone. He admits the photos are not from 2013 and he was not upset in them (one was him playing a game at a church retreat, one eating ice cream). That is the picture book effect: the text tells the story, the visual amplifies the emotion.
- Pursuit lines: Until I finally took accountability... / I cut out distractions (with a small time stamp 6:45 am drive to work) / 4:50 am morning walk / 6:00 am major objectives list / numbered wins: 1. Mental win, 2. Physical win, 4. Honest accountability win. Slide note: must be readable.
- Payoff lines arrive with the music drop: Graduated with my bachelor's and master's in biomedical engineering / Paid off my family's lifelong debts / Started a 7 figure business from 0 dollars / 2024 / 1. I became smarter, 3. I became closer to God. Then reflection and lessons, then a black frame with the CTA (follow to design a winning life).
- Line budget slide: problem 7 lines, pursuit 7 lines, payoff 5 lines, reflect plus lessons 2 plus 2 lines, CTA 1 line. Jun says the exact numbers matter less than the ratio: the payoff is shorter than problem and pursuit, and the order is what makes it work (I resonate, then I learn, then I know why I stayed).
- Typography: small white sans text centred in the middle third, two lines at most, three dots to carry a thought into the next shot, small time stamps placed lower in the frame.

## 11. Sound

The silent film runs on Skyfall. Jun points at the build (up, up, up, then down) and says the payoff starts when the music drops. Measured on his example (about 77 s): the track sits around minus 22 LUFS under the problem lines, falls to near silence twice (around 29 to 31 s and 39 s) right before the story turns, and the payoff and reflection run at the louder sections after it. The story turn and the music turn are cut to the same moment.

## 12. From moment to post (1:02 to 1:04)

Moment: hosted an in-person mastermind in London for about 20 people. Why 1: I want to help others grow. Why 2: I want to build something I can be proud of. Why 3 (raw): I want to prove that people like me, immigrant, poor upbringing, can build something without being handed it. 3 Ps: problem, people think immigrants are stealing their jobs; pursuit, becoming an expert, sharing knowledge, hosting events, building a business; payoff, creating jobs for other people. Then plugged into a format (non-talking reel). Summary slide: storytelling wheel gives you a raw idea, the 3 Ps give it structure, the format delivers that structure through content.

## 13. Q&A points worth keeping (1:50 to 2:20)

- You do not need a success story; a journey toward a goal, told while it happens, is powerful content.
- Choose which stories to tell. A relationship coach does not post about being a bad partner, but can post about noticing they were less present and using their own method to fix it. Real, human, honest, and it builds trust.
- Storytelling is not oversharing or trauma content. A big ambition works as the problem too (building something hard).
- Tell it from your own evolution, without naming other people. Problem, pursuit, payoff can be built from your own realisations alone.
- It took Jun about a year and a half to see any traction, and another stretch to realise he had built the wrong audience.

## What is not in this video

He promises six templates and shows them only through examples; the written playbooks, hook masterclass and AI storytelling coach are paid extras and are not available to us. He says AI should never write the story for you, only help you find it: the raw part has to come from something that really happened.
